# Selected installations in older houses

_English translation of the Czech original._

Consumption figures are read from the heat pump's electricity meter for the first full heating season, including hot water. The customers agreed to publication without their names or exact addresses.

## Family house from 1936, Chrudim area

- **House:** brick, 150 m², ceiling insulated in 2012, windows replaced, original facade.
- **Previous heating:** gas boiler, cast-iron radiators.
- **Heat loss:** 8.5 kW.
- **Solution:** HW-12, two radiators replaced (bathroom, hallway).
- **Flow water at −12 °C:** 52 °C.
- **Consumption:** previously 2,150 m³ of gas a year, with the heat pump 6,900 kWh of electricity a year.

## Family house from 1978, near Hradec Králové

- **House:** brick, 120 m², insulated in 2015.
- **Previous heating:** solid fuel boiler, underfloor heating on the ground floor, radiators upstairs.
- **Heat loss:** 5.8 kW.
- **Solution:** HW-8 with no changes to the heating system.
- **Flow water at −12 °C:** 45 °C.
- **Consumption:** previously 12 stacked cubic metres of wood a year, with the heat pump 3,900 kWh of electricity a year.

## House from 1905, Orlické Mountains foothills – we advised against a heat pump

- **House:** stone walls 70 cm thick, uninsulated, original windows, 190 m².
- **Heat loss:** 19 kW.
- **Recommendation:** insulate the ceiling and replace the windows first. According to the calculation, the heat loss will drop to about 11 kW, and only then does a heat pump make sense (HW-12). Until then we recommended keeping the existing gas boiler.
